Fitness tools

Work out your numbers.

Three free calculators and a few plain guides. Nothing is saved and you do not need an account.

Tool 01

BMI

Checks your weight against your height, and tells you what to do next.

What BMI does and does not tell you

BMI compares your weight to your height. It is a quick check, nothing more. It cannot tell muscle from fat, so someone who lifts can show as “overweight” while being perfectly healthy.

Use it as a starting point. Your waist measurement, how your clothes fit and your blood work all say more. If your BMI is above 30 or below 18.5, talk to a doctor as well as a trainer.

Tool 02

Calories and protein

How much to eat a day to lose fat, stay the same, or gain muscle.

Eating on normal home food

You do not need imported powders or a separate kitchen. Most people get where they want on the food already at home, in different amounts.

  • Put a protein in every meal: dal, curd, paneer, eggs, chicken or soya.
  • Keep the roti and rice. Just measure them instead of guessing.
  • Add a vegetable or salad to lunch and dinner so you feel full.
  • Drink water, not sweet drinks. Most extra calories are liquid.
  • Eat the same way six days out of seven. The seventh does not matter.

General guidance, not medical advice. If you are pregnant, diabetic, on medication or managing a health condition, check with your doctor first.

Tool 03

One-rep max

Works out your max lift from a set you already did, and the weights to train with.

How to use these numbers

Most of your training should sit between 70% and 85% of your max. Heavy enough to build muscle, light enough that your form holds for every rep.

Add weight when you can finish all your sets with a rep or two left. Two and a half kilos on a big lift is real progress and adds up over a few months.

Never try a true one-rep max alone. Ask a trainer and use a spotter.

First week

What happens on day one.

  • Bring shoes, a towel and a water bottle. That is all.
  • A trainer shows you round and explains each machine.
  • You get a starting routine, about 45 minutes.
  • Expect to be sore for two or three days. That is normal.
  • Come back the next day even if you are sore. Turning up matters more than going hard.

The first month is about turning up. Three sessions a week for four weeks does more than one hard week followed by nothing.

Common questions

Straight answers.

01How long before I see results?

You will feel stronger and sleep better in two to three weeks. Visible change usually takes eight to twelve weeks of steady training and eating.

02Do I need supplements?

No. Food first. If you cannot hit your protein target from meals, a basic whey protein helps. Nothing else is needed.

03Cardio or weights for fat loss?

Both, with weights first. Weights keep your muscle while you lose fat, so you look better at the same weight. Cardio and daily walking burn the extra calories.

04Is it bad to train every day?

Muscles grow while you rest. Three to five sessions a week with rest days works better than seven. Walk on the off days if you want to move.

05I am over 50. Is weight training safe?

Yes, and it is one of the best things you can do for bone strength and balance. We start you on machines with light weight. Tell us about any medical condition and clear it with your doctor.

06Will lifting make women bulky?

No. Building a lot of muscle takes years of deliberate effort and a lot of food. Lifting will make you stronger and more toned.

Everything on this page is general fitness information, not medical advice. If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or take medication, speak to your doctor before starting a new training or diet plan.
